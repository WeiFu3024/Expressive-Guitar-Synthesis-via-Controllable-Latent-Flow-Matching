Expressive Guitar Synthesis via Controllable Latent Flow Matching

Realistic guitar synthesis requires both audio fidelity and interpretable control over how notes are performed. Current systems achieve one or the other: DDSP-based methods trade synthesis quality for controllability and data efficiency, and vice versa for diffusion-based models. We propose a model that separates control from rendering: a predictor estimates per-string performance attributes from MIDI as an editable intermediate, which a latent flow-matching decoder renders into audio. Two mechanisms make this design practical: a conditioning encoder that re-encodes the predicted attributes together with exactly known symbolic cues, so the decoder tolerates imperfect predictions, and gated cross-string attention, which models the six strings jointly rather than independently. Objective and subjective evaluations show that our system matches the quality of diffusion-based baselines while retaining explicit control over performance attributes.
